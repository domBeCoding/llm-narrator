# The Herbalist's Choice

> A short interactive story for the LLM Narrator prototype.
> Genre: Low fantasy / village drama. Estimated play time: 10-15 minutes.

---

## Story World

### Setting
The village of **Mossbank** — a small, isolated settlement nestled between a slow-moving river and the edge of the **Greymere Forest**. The village has ~200 residents. It's autumn. The harvest has been poor. People are anxious.

Key locations:
- **The Apothecary** — a small stone cottage where the reader lives and works with their mentor. Dried herbs hang from the rafters. It smells of lavender and earth.
- **The Village Square** — the social heart of Mossbank. A well, a notice board, a few market stalls. Where gossip spreads.
- **The Greymere Forest** — dense, old-growth forest to the north. Villagers avoid it. Stories of people entering and not returning. Strictly forbidden by village elders.
- **Halden's Cottage** — a secluded home at the forest's edge, occupied by a reclusive old woman. Villagers whisper that she's a witch.

### Tone
Warm but tinged with unease. The village feels like home, but the forest looms. The story should feel intimate — small stakes, big emotional weight.

---

## Characters

### The Reader (First Person)
- **Name**: Wren (default, reader can change)
- **Role**: Apprentice herbalist
- **Background**: Orphaned at age 7, taken in by Master Elara. Has lived in the apothecary since. Knows herbs, remedies, and basic medicine. Doesn't know magic. Has never left Mossbank.
- **Personality (baseline)**: Caring, curious, slightly anxious. Deeply loyal to Elara. Self-doubting about their own abilities.
- **Relationships**:
 - **Elara**: Mentor, surrogate mother. Deep bond.
 - **Tomas**: Childhood friend. Easy warmth, unspoken tension.
 - **Halden**: Unknown. Only heard village rumors.

### Master Elara
- **Role**: Village herbalist and healer. The reader's mentor.
- **Age**: 60s
- **Personality**: Gentle, wise, dry humor. Patient teacher. Hides worry well. Has a stubborn streak — refuses to ask for help.
- **Secret**: She once entered the Greymere Forest in her youth and survived. She never talks about it.
- **State through story**: Healthy → critically ill → recovering
- **Voice**: Calm, measured, uses metaphors from nature. Even when frightened, she speaks softly.

### Tomas
- **Role**: Village woodcutter's son. Reader's childhood friend.
- **Age**: Same as reader (~20)
- **Personality**: Warm, practical, protective. Not scholarly. Acts before thinking. Secretly in love with the reader but won't say it.
- **State through story**: Supportive → worried → relieved
- **Voice**: Direct, casual, uses contractions. Drops into earnest sincerity when emotional.

### Halden
- **Role**: Reclusive old woman living at the forest's edge.
- **Age**: 80s
- **Personality**: Sharp-tongued, suspicious of outsiders. Not actually a witch — she's a retired herbalist who left village life decades ago after a dispute with the elders. Knows the forest well.
- **Secret**: She knows Elara. They were friends in their youth. The falling out was about the forest — Elara wanted to forget, Halden wanted to study it.
- **State through story**: Hostile → grudgingly helpful → emotionally reconciled with Elara (if reader facilitates)
- **Voice**: Curt, sarcastic, uses old-fashioned phrasing. Softens only when caught off guard by genuine emotion.

### Village Elder Corwin
- **Role**: Village elder. Authority figure.
- **Personality**: Strict, traditional, risk-averse. Genuinely cares about the village but prioritizes safety over individuals. Enforces the forest ban.
- **Voice**: Formal, authoritative, uses "we" when he means "I".

## Story Structure

### Act 1 — The Start (World Building & Setup)

**Scene 1: Morning in the Apothecary**

The reader wakes in the apothecary. It's an ordinary morning — or should be. Elara is late to rise, which is unlike her. The reader goes about morning routines (sorting herbs, preparing the shop) until they check on Elara and find her feverish and barely conscious.

**World building delivered through**: The morning routine itself — the reader interacts with familiar objects, the village sounds outside, the relationship with Elara shown through small details (her favorite tea, the way she organizes her shelves).

**Reader actions available**:
- Examine Elara's symptoms
- Search the apothecary for relevant remedies
- Go to the village square to ask for help
- Check Elara's private journal (she'd hate this, but it's available)

**Key revelation**: Elara's illness doesn't match any common ailment. The reader finds a torn page in an old book — this matches symptoms of **Greymere Fever**, a condition treatable only with **Moonpetal**, a flower that grows exclusively deep in the Greymere Forest.

**State at end of Act 1**:
- Reader: alarmed, uncertain, newly aware of the forest's relevance
- Elara: critically ill, bedridden
- Tomas: unaware of situation
- Halden: unaware of situation
- World: ordinary village morning shattered by crisis

---

### Act 2 — The Middle (The Dilemma)

**Scene 2: The Crossroads**

The reader now faces the central dilemma. Elara is getting worse. The cure is in the forbidden forest. The elders enforce a strict ban. The reader must choose a path.

**Three main approaches** (the reader can blend or attempt alternatives):

**Path A — Enter the Forest Alone**
The reader sneaks into the Greymere Forest. This is dangerous — the forest is disorienting, old, and hostile to those who don't know it. The reader would be relying on luck and basic herbal knowledge.

- Risk: Getting lost, injured, or not finding the Moonpetal in time
- The LLM should generate an atmospheric, tense forest experience
- If the reader finds Halden's cottage first, Halden may intervene

**Path B — Seek Halden's Help**
The reader goes to Halden's cottage at the forest's edge. Halden knows the forest. But she's hostile to outsiders and has history with Elara that the reader doesn't fully understand.

- Challenge: Persuading Halden to help. She's bitter, suspicious, and initially refuses.
- Opportunity: If the reader is empathetic and persistent, Halden reveals her connection to Elara and agrees to guide them into the forest — or provides the Moonpetal herself
- Risk: Halden may turn the reader away. Time is wasting.

**Path C — Appeal to Elder Corwin**
The reader goes to Elder Corwin and asks for official permission to enter the forest, or for help.

- Challenge: Corwin refuses. The ban is absolute. He may even threaten to confine the reader to the village to prevent them from trying.
- This path is a dead end on its own, but it can inform the reader about *why* the forest is banned (old incidents, fear) and add pressure.
- A clever reader might use this to guilt Corwin later, or to involve Tomas.

**Tomas as a variable**: At any point during Act 2, the reader can go to Tomas. Tomas will want to help. He might:
- Offer to go into the forest with the reader (brave but reckless)
- Suggest talking to Halden (he's heard stories)
- Try to talk the reader out of it (out of fear for their safety)

**State at end of Act 2**:
- Reader: has committed to a path, is under time pressure
- Elara: deteriorating — may become delirious, murmuring names from her past (including Halden's)
- Tomas: involved or not, depending on reader choice
- Halden: encountered or not, depending on reader choice
- Tension: high — the clock is ticking

---

### Act 3 — The End (Resolution)

**Scene 3: The Moonpetal**

The reader obtains the Moonpetal (through whichever path they took). The experience of getting it should reflect the path — solo forest traversal is tense and dangerous; with Halden it's a journey of discovery and reconciliation; with Tomas it's about trust and partnership.

**Key emotional beat**: If Halden is present, she and Elara reunite. This is the emotional climax — two old friends, separated by pride and fear, brought back together by the reader's determination. Halden enters the apothecary. Elara, feverish, recognizes her. A quiet, powerful moment.

**Scene 4: The Cure and Aftermath**

The reader prepares the Moonpetal remedy. Elara recovers over the next few days. The story ends on a warm, quiet morning — mirroring the opening scene but transformed. The apothecary is the same, but the reader has changed. They've proven their capability. The world feels a little more open.

**Possible endings (all happy, but varied)**:
- **If Halden reconciled with Elara**: Halden visits regularly now. The apothecary has three people in it. Elara teaches again. The reader has two mentors.
- **If Tomas helped**: Tomas and the reader share a quiet moment. Their relationship has deepened. He says something earnest. The reader feels seen.
- **If the reader went alone**: The reader sits with Elara as she recovers. She looks at them with new respect. "You don't need me to watch over you anymore," she says. The reader feels the bittersweet weight of growing up.
- **If the reader involved Corwin**: Corwin visits, grudgingly impressed. The forest ban is quietly relaxed. The village feels less afraid.

**State at end of Act 3**:
- Reader: confident, proven, emotionally grown
- Elara: recovering, proud of the reader
- Halden: reintegrated (if path taken) or still isolated
- Tomas: closer to reader (if involved) or unaware of the full story
- World: Mossbank feels the same, but the reader sees it differently

## State Tracking Reference

The following tables show how character and world state should evolve through the story. These are intended as a reference for the narration system — the LLM should consult these to maintain consistency.

### Reader State

| Phase | Emotional State | Knowledge | Relationships |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 Start | Content, routine | Knows herbs, village life | Close to Elara, friendly with Tomas |
| Act 1 End | Alarmed, uncertain | Knows about Greymere Fever, Moonpetal | Same, but worried about Elara |
| Act 2 | Determined, anxious | Knows the forest is key, may know about Halden | May have deepened connection with Tomas or Halden |
| Act 3 | Relieved, proud | Has grown in skill and self-knowledge | Transformed — new respect from Elara, possibly closer to Tomas/Halden |

### Elara State

| Phase | Health | Conscious | Key Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 Start | Healthy | Yes | Normal routine |
| Act 1 End | Critical fever | Barely | Weak, trying to downplay severity |
| Act 2 | Deteriorating | Fading in and out | May murmur Halden's name in delirium |
| Act 3 | Recovering | Yes | Proud, emotional, grateful |

### Halden State

| Phase | Disposition | Key Information Revealed |
|---|---|---|
| Before encounter | Hostile, suspicious | None |
| First encounter | Curt, dismissive | That she knows the forest |
| If persuaded | Grudgingly helpful | That she knows Elara |
| During forest trip | Opening up, nostalgic | Her history with Elara, why she left |
| At apothecary | Vulnerable, emotional | Full reconciliation with Elara |

### Tomas State

| Phase | Emotional State | Action Available |
|---|---|---|
| Before involvement | Unaware | None |
| When told | Worried, protective | Offer to help, try to dissuade, suggest Halden |
| During involvement | Brave, earnest | Accompany reader, provide practical support |
| After resolution | Relieved, emotionally open | Express feelings (subtly) |

## Author Notes

- The story is deliberately small in scope. One village, a handful of characters, one central dilemma. This is intentional — it should be completable in a single short session and serve as a test bed for the narration system.
- The three paths (forest alone, Halden, Corwin) are not mutually exclusive. A reader might try Corwin first, get refused, then go to Halden. The system should support this kind of path-blending.
- Tomas is a social safety valve — he's always available as someone the reader can talk to, process emotions with, and get practical help from. He prevents the reader from feeling stuck.
- The emotional climax is the Halden-Elara reunion, but it's optional. The system should make the story satisfying regardless of which path is taken.
- The opening and closing scenes mirror each other (morning in the apothecary) to give the story a sense of bookending and transformation.
